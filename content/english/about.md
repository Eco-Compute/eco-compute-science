**ecoCompute Science** is a peer-reviewed venue for resource aware and resource reducing
computing. It runs online in **summer 2027**.

The short version: every submission ships a runnable artifact, the write-up is two pages,
the reviewers run the thing, and the event itself is discussion only.

## The problem we are trying to fix

With the rise of LLMs, writing a long paper is no longer evidence that work was done.
Producing numbers that somebody else can reproduce is.

The ecoCompute conference has shown that a *show me the numbers* culture works in practice.
What is missing is a peer-reviewed venue that holds research to the same standard: one where
the reviewers actually run the artifact instead of judging the prose that describes it.

## Scope

Resource aware and resource reducing computing. That includes, and is not limited to:

- Energy and carbon measurement of software and hardware
- Efficiency work: compilers, runtimes, schedulers, architectures, data centre operations
- Carbon accounting and attribution methodologies
- Hardware lifetime, embodied emissions, repair and reuse
- Memory, storage and bandwidth reduction
- The measurement methods themselves, including their uncertainty

If the work cannot be opened to reviewers in any form, it is out of scope. See
[Artifacts](/artifacts) for what "opened to reviewers" means in practice.

## Three things we do differently

### 1. Artifact first

Every submission ships a complete, runnable artifact. A ZIP file or a container image is
fine, no hosting platform is required. What is required is a precise, step-by-step guide
that lets a reviewer regenerate the numbers in the submission, or otherwise verify its
claims, without asking the authors for help.

If the work needs special hardware, authors must be able to give reviewers access to those
systems.

Because energy and carbon measurements are not bit-reproducible, authors state explicitly
what is supposed to be stable, an ordering, a direction, an effect size, and within what
tolerance. They report the variance of their own runs rather than a single mean.

### 2. Two pages, bullet points welcome

The write-up is a map to the artifact, not the evidence itself. Submissions are at most two
pages and consist of:

- What I want to show
- How I showed it
- What the results are
- Prior work
- What I want to discuss

Good bullet points are entirely acceptable. Prose is not rewarded.

### 3. No talks

The event is discussion only, adapted from the session format used at
[ICT4S](https://ict4s.org/). Nobody presents slides to a silent room.

## What we especially want to see

- **Negative results.** The measurement did not show what you expected. The optimisation did
  not pay off. The effect disappeared once you controlled for the noise.
- **Replication studies.** "We tried to reproduce X and could not" is a first-class
  submission here, not second-tier filler. An artifact-centred venue is its natural home.
- **Methodology and accounting work**, where the artifact is the calculation applied to
  sample setups rather than a piece of software.
- **Tools and measurement stacks**, with the evidence that they do what they say.

## How the event runs

- Online, European timezone. There is no venue.
- Each accepted paper gets three sessions. The author takes part in every one of them.
- Participants register for individual sessions in advance. Group size is capped so that
  everyone can actually speak.
- The two-pagers circulate a week ahead. Having read them is a condition of taking part.
- Session notes, generated by an LLM, go back to the author and become input for the full
  paper.

The event is a processing step between artifact and publication, not a showcase for finished
work.

## Publication

Two stages. Stage one is the two-pager plus artifact, which is reviewed, accepted and
presented at the event. Stage two is a full paper, written after the event and shepherded by
a committee member who confirms that the claims did not outgrow the evidence that was
actually reviewed. The full paper receives a DOI.

See [Review and Publication](/review) for the detail.

## Relationship to the ecoCompute conference

ecoCompute Science grows out of the [ecoCompute conference](/previous-years), held in Munich
in 2024 and Berlin in 2025. Because of the overhead of organising a large in-person event,
there will be no in-person ecoCompute in 2027. The talks, speakers and schedules of both
conferences stay online.
