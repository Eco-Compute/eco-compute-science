Review at ecoCompute Science is done by people who run the artifact. Everything else follows
from that.

## The review process

### 1. Submission

Two pages plus a complete, runnable artifact. See the
[Call for Papers](/call-for-papers) and [Artifacts](/artifacts).

### 2. Kick the tires

The first days of the review period are a setup phase. Reviewers try to get the artifact
running and report anything that blocks them from starting. Authors get a short window to
fix it.

Artifacts should not fail on setup problems. A missing dependency is not a scientific
judgement and should not be treated as one.

### 3. Artifact evaluation

A dedicated artifact evaluation committee runs the submissions. The committee checks whether
the claim, as the authors stated it, holds when somebody else executes the artifact:

- Does the guide work end to end on a machine that is not the authors'?
- Do the numbers in the two-pager come out of the artifact?
- Does the stated stable property, the ordering, direction or effect size, hold within the
  stated tolerance?
- Is the reported variance consistent with what the reviewer sees?

### 4. Outcome, published

Review outcome and artifact status are published together with the paper. Readers see
whether an artifact was reproduced, partially reproduced or not reproduced, and why.

A paper whose artifact only partially reproduced is not automatically a failure. A paper
that hides which parts did not reproduce is.

### 5. The event

Accepted two-pagers are discussed at the online event. Three sessions per paper, capped
group size, author present in every session, everybody has read the two-pagers a week in
advance. Session notes are generated by an LLM and sent back to the author.

## Publication in two stages

### Stage one: the two-pager plus artifact

Reviewed, accepted and presented at the event. This is the version the community discusses.

### Stage two: the full paper

Written after the event, using the session notes as input. A committee member shepherds it.

The shepherd has one specific job on top of the usual: **confirming that the claims did not
outgrow the evidence that was actually reviewed.** A full paper that quietly widens a result
from "on this hardware, under this workload" to "in general" does not pass shepherding.

The full paper receives a DOI. The journal hosting stage two is being finalised.
Anna-Lena Lamprecht is handling the publication track.

## On writing quality

A good idea and a working artifact do not guarantee a paper somebody can read, and an LLM
does not fix that on its own. Shepherding at stage two covers structure and clarity as well
as claim discipline. If English is not your first language, say so and we will pair you with
a shepherd who can help rather than penalise it.

At stage one, the two-pager is deliberately short and bullet points are fine, so writing
skill is a much smaller barrier than at a normal venue. That is on purpose.

## Committees

We are assembling two committees:

- A **programme committee**, responsible for scope, acceptance and shepherding.
- An **artifact evaluation committee**, responsible for running the artifacts.

If you want to review, [get in touch](/contact). Tell us what you can run and what hardware
you have access to. Reviewers get named in the published outcome unless they ask not to be.

## Open questions on review

These are genuinely undecided and we would like input before we fix them.

**How do we keep artifact review affordable?**
Artifact review is expensive, far more expensive than reading a PDF. The options on the
table are a cap on submissions, rolling reviews, a first reviewer who produces a working
recipe that the others then follow, and AI assistance for the first reproduction pass. If an
artifact is genuinely reproducible by anyone who read the paper and has the domain
knowledge, that first pass is a good candidate for automation. The judgement stays with
people.

**Single-blind or fully open?**
Artifacts are very hard to anonymise well. Repository names, author machines, internal paths
and licence headers all leak. We have to decide whether to accept single-blind review or go
fully open and stop pretending.

**Confidential industry data.**
If a reviewer cannot see the data, reproducibility in the usual sense is out of reach, and
synthetic sample data is often not realistic enough to be worth much. The planned industry
and impact track is our current answer. See the
[Call for Papers](/call-for-papers).

**No-shows.**
Free registration produces high no-show rates, which is fatal for capped discussion groups.
A small fee, a three-sentence application, or a Dagstuhl-style invitation model where you
would not want to let down the person who invited you. All are being considered.
